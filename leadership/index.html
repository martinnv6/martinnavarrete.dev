DellRewards & ShareCart

An example of leading through influence: no one in the room reported to me.

Situation

Bringing loyalty rewards into the unified dell.com cart touched the Pricing, Account, Memberships and Items domains, plus legal and tax rules that differ by country.

What I did

Over 6 months and about 30 meetings with those teams' architects and engineers, alongside legal and tax, I worked through the architecture and design disagreements until we reached a multilateral agreement on one robust, multi-country solution.

  • 35,000+ registered customers by month 8

Dell · Developer tooling

AI agents adopted, not mandated

The agent does the repeatable steps (clone, branch, Jira fields, moving the ticket to In Development, first analysis) and drafts the pull request. The engineer reviews it, decides and owns the result. I shared it as a skills repository instead of a rule, and adoption came from the time it saved.

On the vendor side, I wrote statements of work for CEMEX: stages, epics, deliverables with acceptance criteria, exclusions, and capped time-and-materials with the seniority mix I proposed, with IP, access and security settled before work started. How I'd approach it

A platform initiative in seven steps

  1. Understand and baselineTalk to developers, map the friction, measure where each team starts.
  2. Prioritize by friction and reachFix what slows the most people first.
  3. Minimal version with volunteersShip a thin golden path with teams that opt in.
  4. Adoption as a funnelTrack who knows it, tries it, adopts it and stays.
  5. Outcomes and experienceDORA and DevEx metrics, each speed metric paired with a quality counter-metric.
  6. Scale by influenceChampions teach the platform; teams use it because it helps.
  7. Govern and reportOwners, versions, responsible-AI rules and a quarterly report in business terms.
